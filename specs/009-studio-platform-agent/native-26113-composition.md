## Open ownership and authority

The original accepted-request/reply/destructor and hardware callbacks still need a fully reviewed all-client boundary. In particular the idle warm-up queue can outlive its load caller. The checked local slot/twin handoff is not a live-parent handle for queued producer work. Keep this producer uncovered until a bounded original handle can be acquired before queue publication, survive pickup, and be returned only after checked retirement or retained on refusal. A token copied after the original response is not a substitute. `reservePreparation` binds its child to the model under the tracker lock, so reusing it for every idle obligation would pin otherwise-evictable models indefinitely while the server is busy. The future handoff needs an exact never-dispatched queue withdrawal that relinquishes only that child, a finite original lease, and a distinct acquired worker state. Do not pick one token out of a grouped parent or let lease expiry retire a slot that may have dispatched. The source-independent contract in `tests/platform-idle-warm-lineage-model.test.js` treats queue withdrawal and pickup as mutually exclusive results for one exact root, job and process epoch; deadline expiry is not itself withdrawal. A production bridge must also account for verify-width sweeps (no slot is published for them) and retain custody through cache destruction, not just until the last chat slot exits. Do not reuse a completeness claim from the model as a native observation.

The handoff cannot be implemented by calling `retainCurrent` from `tick`: `retainTokens` accepts still-checked responding tokens and increments references on the original model-bearing slot. This is both too late and can change model mutation eligibility. Instead evaluate a bounded, registry-independent queued child tied to the complete incoming lineage before `owe`; bind its exact entry load claim and a model guard only at pickup; keep the guard across the width sweep or chat slot and cache release. Before changing a native path, verify registry → tracker ordering at both `forget` callers and queue-timeout wakeups: `owesStepLocked` currently requires the queue to be otherwise idle, so it cannot by itself schedule expiry. `step.zig:chatPass(.main)` ultimately parks at an untimed `queue_cond.waitUncancelable`; depend on neither periodic traffic nor its unrelated disk retry timers for expiry. The timeout must wake the inference thread to attempt only exact withdrawal; the clock alone grants no cleanup right. A worker or owner claim that cannot be checked must leave original custody intact. An acquired model guard cannot rely on a later `forget` to drop it: `beginModelMutation` can refuse before either unload caller reaches `forget`. Qualify a bounded voluntary relinquishment of the model guard only after a checked step (while a separate original-lineage ticket owns any between-step queue work and cache), or use a reviewed original-ticket cancellation edge before mutation. Do not retry or evict around a disputed guard. The local tracker prototype carries complete correlation after the original model-bound parents leave. Keep those queue tokens private, and issue **fresh worker tokens per acquired step**: changing only a step number in a reused token would reject an old explicit receipt but could not distinguish a late generic callback that still holds the old token. Twin destruction during eviction also needs a checked lineage scope tied to the original mutation, rather than a second model guard that conflicts with it. `HotPrefixCache.deinit` and `plugin_driver.auxFree` currently return `void`; surrounding negative probes do not show which subtree retired if a destructor fences midway. Keep that path uncovered until an exact checked boundary exists. For chat dispatch, `submitWaiting` clones a nonzero registry borrow before `Slot.init` retains lineage; an acquired tracker guard cannot stand in for the independent registry borrow. A queued item that has not dispatched must remain eligible for ordinary model eviction.

Layer 73 stages the fallible MTP-head, late drafter-path and hot-prefix allocations before transferring ownership to `LoadedModel`. It distinguishes pre-transfer errors, successfully published readiness, and a non-error post-transfer hold (which must not run pre-transfer `errdefer` cleanup). Primary startup and queued load acknowledge that hold as `NativeCleanupUncertain`, retaining the original model, heap twin, slot and reservation. The warm marker is persisted only after readiness is published. Model-free controls cover the three provisional allocation failures, checked heap-cache retirement/refusal, and the queued refusal’s original claim and retained charge. These are local checks, not backend terminality or a production rehearsal.
